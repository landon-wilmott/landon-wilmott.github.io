EC EN 340 Power Integrity Virtual Ground NGspice

Artifact 1: Power Supply Rail Splitter & 3-Stage Ripple Decoupling Network

  • The Problem: Our optical receiver was powered by a single $3.3\text{V}$ DC source (V1) contaminated with a $30\text{mV}$ ($1481\text{Hz}$) AC ripple—directly inside our $1250\text{Hz}\text{–}3666\text{Hz}$ passband—while the op-amps required clean, symmetric $+1.65\text{V}$ (vp) and $-1.65\text{V}$ (vn) dual rails.
  • My Contribution: Designed a 3-stage cascaded RC low-pass decoupling filter ($R = 10\ \Omega, C = 159\ \mu\text{F}, f_c \approx 100\text{Hz}$) feeding a matched $100\text{k}\Omega$ voltage divider (R2/R3). Strategically placed the reference ground (0V) between R2 and R3 with parallel $1\text{mF}$ bulk and $1\ \mu\text{F}$ bypass capacitors.
  • The Result: Generated rock-solid $v_p = +1.65\text{V}$ and $v_n = -1.65\text{V}$ rails while heavily attenuating the $1481\text{Hz}$ supply ripple before it could modulate the high-gain amplifier stages.
  • What This Demonstrates: Power integrity engineering, virtual ground synthesis, multi-decade capacitor bypassing, and constraint-driven SPICE modeling.

EC EN 340 Active Bandpass 120 dB Gain Bode Design

Artifact 2: Dual Op-Amp Cascaded Bandpass Noise Filter (1.25–3.67 kHz)

  • The Problem: Using at most two op-amps, isolate a tiny $1\text{pA}$ ($2000\text{Hz}$) photodiode signal (Is) buried under a $1\text{mA}$ DC offset (In1), two $400\text{nA}$ noise sources at $100\text{Hz}$ (In2) and $20\text{kHz}$ (In3), and a $1481\text{Hz}$ power ripple so only the $1250\text{Hz}\text{–}3666\text{Hz}$ band passes.
  • My Contribution: Architected a hybrid filter chain with lower corner $f_L = 1000\text{Hz}$ and upper corner $f_H = 10\text{kHz}$: a 3-stage passive RC high-pass input filter ($15\text{nF}/10.6\text{k}\Omega$), two cascaded inverting bandpass op-amps ($R_{in}=1\text{k}\Omega, C_{in}=159\text{nF}, R_f=1\text{M}\Omega, C_f=15.9\text{pF}$, yielding $60\text{dB}$ gain/stage), and a 3-stage passive RC low-pass output filter ($1.6\text{k}\Omega/10\text{nF}$).
  • The Result: Achieved $120\text{dB}$ ($10^6\text{ V/V}$) passband amplification across $1250\text{Hz}\text{–}3666\text{Hz}$ while sharply attenuating DC, $100\text{Hz}$, and $20\text{kHz}$ noise without rail saturation.
  • What This Demonstrates: Multi-pole filter synthesis, corner-frequency calculation, high-gain cascaded amplifier staging, and SNR optimization.

EC EN 340 Single-Supply 9V Robotics Sensor AC Coupling

Artifact 3: Single-Supply Light-Following Robot Filter & Virtual Ground Biasing

  • The Problem: Process a $1\text{kHz}$ pulsed optical beacon (I1 = 0.1 mA) for a light-following robot operating from a single $9\text{V}$ battery (V1) and $0\text{V}$ ground, while filtering out ambient room lighting that would otherwise clip the signal against the $0\text{V}$ rail.
  • My Contribution: Designed an AC-coupled inverting amplifier ($R_4 = 10\text{M}\Omega, R_5 = 50\text{M}\Omega, |A_v| = 5\text{ V/V}$) with op-amp rails at $9\text{V}$ and $0\text{V}$, using a matched $100\text{k}\Omega$ divider (R7/R8) to bias the non-inverting input (+) to a $+4.5\text{V}$ virtual ground, paired with input ($C_1 = 1\ \mu\text{F}$) and output ($C_2 = 1\ \mu\text{F}, R_9 = 1\text{M}\Omega$) DC-blocking stages.
  • The Result: Enabled full symmetric AC swing centered at $+4.5\text{V}$ inside the op-amp without clipping, and restored a clean $0\text{V}$-centered output waveform (out) immune to ambient room light.
  • What This Demonstrates: Single-supply op-amp biasing, DC blocking / AC restoration, and practical embedded sensor conditioning.

EC EN 340 SPICE Netlist Debugging & Audit Iterative Revision

Artifact 4: NGspice Netlist Verification, Capacitor Routing Audit & Revision

  • The Problem: Early iterations of the optical receiver netlist suffered from signal loss and insufficient out-of-band roll-off due to mis-grounded coupling capacitors (C6/C7, C23/C24) and strict requirements to preserve starter components (V1, I1, R1) unchanged.
  • My Contribution: Conducted a node-by-node netlist audit to route all coupling capacitors strictly in series without grounding them, verified shunt bypass capacitor terminations at node 0, preserved starter V1/I1/R1 interconnections, and upgraded single-pole filters into 3-stage RC cascades based on feedback.
  • The Result: Eliminated schematic-to-netlist discrepancies, achieved steep 4th-order roll-off outside the $1\text{kHz}\text{–}10\text{kHz}$ window, and passed 100% of Milestone 1 simulation checks.
  • What This Demonstrates: Systematic root-cause debugging, SPICE netlist fluency, strict interface compliance, and feedback-driven engineering iteration.

Iterative Improvement

Evidence of Revision Based on Feedback

Both my circuit designs and this engineering portfolio underwent structured revisions following instructor, TA, and peer feedback:

  • Portfolio Framing & Navigation Revision: Earlier versions of my site presented raw procedural notes (labeled simply "Context" and "Procedure") without a clear target role or site-wide navigation. Based on portfolio feedback, I added a focused Target Role (Security Infrastructure & Data Center Hardware Engineer), an organized Table of Contents & Sticky Navigation Bar, and restructured every artifact into The Problem → My Contribution → The Result → What This Artifact Demonstrates.
  • Coupling Capacitor & Virtual Ground Node Revision (Artifacts 1 & 4): Initial schematic drafts risked grounding coupling capacitors (C6/C7) or referencing single-supply op-amps directly to 0V, causing severe rail clipping. Following simulation diagnostics and lab feedback, I placed the reference ground between matched $100\text{k}\Omega$ divider resistors (R2/R3 for $\pm 1.65\text{V}$ rails; R7/R8 for $+4.5\text{V}$ biasing) and routed coupling capacitors strictly in series.
  • Multi-Pole Roll-Off Upgrade (Artifact 2): When single-pole filtering allowed residual $100\text{Hz}$ and $20\text{kHz}$ photodiode noise to bleed into the $1250\text{Hz}\text{–}3666\text{Hz}$ window, I revised the architecture by adding 3-stage cascaded passive RC high-pass ($f_L \approx 1\text{kHz}$) and low-pass ($f_H \approx 10\text{kHz}$) sections around the dual active op-amp stages.
